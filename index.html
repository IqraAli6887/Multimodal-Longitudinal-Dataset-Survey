Why LoMED

Longitudinal learning is advancing faster than longitudinal evaluation.

Repeated observations create distinct requirements around chronology, subject dependence, missingness, cross-modal alignment, uncertainty, and transfer. LoMED audits whether the evidence reported in each study matches the form of generalization it claims.

Corpus-wide prevalence shows which validation practices are reported. Claim-conditioned coverage asks a stricter question: when a validation dimension is actually motivated by a study’s coded claim or challenge, is it present?

01

Joint evidence is rare

Only 7 studies combine subject and temporal evaluation, 15 combine subject and external evaluation, and none combine temporal and external validation.

02

Handling ≠ robustness

Among 47 studies facing irregular intervals, missing visits, or sparse observations, 31 identify a handling strategy—but only 17 both handle the issue and test robustness.

03

Transparency ≠ temporal validity

Interpretability appears in 56 studies, yet only 4 of those use temporal splits and just 1 conducts prospective validation.

Evaluation guidance

Match evidence to the claim.

LoMED argues against a universal checklist. Instead, validation should be conditioned on the form of longitudinal generalization a study actually claims.

Temporal claimsChronology-preserving evaluationTemporal / horizon splits and leakage checks
Cross-subject claimsSubject-disjoint evaluationSeparate participants or users across splits
Missingness / shift claimsStress testing & robustnessVary observation process, missingness, or domain
Uncertainty claimsCalibration analysisEvaluate probability quality, not only point metrics
Transfer / deployment claimsExternal or prospective validationTest new settings, cohorts, sites, or future deployment
